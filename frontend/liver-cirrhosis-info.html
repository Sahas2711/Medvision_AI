Liver Cirrhosis

Comprehensive information about liver cirrhosis, its causes, symptoms, and treatment options

What is Liver Cirrhosis?

Liver cirrhosis is a late stage of scarring (fibrosis) of the liver caused by many forms of liver diseases and conditions, such as hepatitis and chronic alcoholism. The liver carries out several necessary functions, including detoxifying harmful substances in your body, cleaning your blood, and making vital nutrients.

  • Progressive replacement of healthy liver tissue with scar tissue
  • Irreversible condition that affects liver function
  • Can lead to liver failure if left untreated
  • May progress to liver cancer in some cases

Common Symptoms

  • Fatigue and weakness
  • Loss of appetite and weight loss
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Abdominal pain and swelling
  • Swelling in legs and ankles (edema)
  • Yellowing of skin and eyes (jaundice)
  • Itchy skin
  • Easy bruising and bleeding
  • Confusion and difficulty thinking clearly
  • Spider-like blood vessels on skin

Causes and Risk Factors

  • Chronic alcohol abuse (most common cause)
  • Chronic viral hepatitis (B, C, and D)
  • Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)
  • Autoimmune hepatitis
  • Primary biliary cholangitis
  • Primary sclerosing cholangitis
  • Hemochromatosis (iron buildup)
  • Wilson's disease (copper accumulation)
  • Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency
  • Certain medications and toxins

Diagnostic Methods

  • Blood tests (liver function tests, complete blood count)
  • Imaging studies (ultrasound, CT scan, MRI)
  • Liver biopsy (definitive diagnosis)
  • FibroScan (measures liver stiffness)
  • Endoscopy (to check for varices)
  • AI-powered imaging analysis for early detection

Treatment Options

  • Treating underlying cause (stop alcohol, antiviral therapy)
  • Medications to manage complications
  • Dietary modifications and nutritional support
  • Management of portal hypertension
  • Treatment of ascites and edema
  • Prevention and treatment of infections
  • Liver transplantation (in advanced cases)
  • Regular monitoring and follow-up care

Prevention Strategies

  • Limit or avoid alcohol consumption
  • Maintain a healthy weight
  • Get vaccinated against hepatitis A and B
  • Practice safe sex and avoid sharing needles
  • Use medications responsibly
  • Eat a balanced, low-sodium diet
  • Exercise regularly
  • Regular health screenings and liver function tests

Complications

  • Portal hypertension and varices
  • Ascites (fluid accumulation in abdomen)
  • Hepatic encephalopathy (brain dysfunction)
  • Liver cancer (hepatocellular carcinoma)
  • Kidney failure (hepatorenal syndrome)
  • Increased risk of infections
  • Bone disease and fractures
  • Gallstones

Specialist Consultation

Hepatologist: Liver disease specialist

Gastroenterologist: Digestive system specialist

Transplant Surgeon: For advanced cases requiring transplantation

When to seek help: Persistent abdominal pain, jaundice, swelling, or any concerning symptoms

Emergency signs: Severe abdominal pain, vomiting blood, confusion, or difficulty breathing
